[TITLE: Terms and Conditions]
[GENRE: Alternative Hip-Hop / Acoustic Folk / Spoken Word]

[MOOD: Ironic, bureaucratic, wounded, socially critical → warmer, defiant, self-accepting]

[TEMPO: 86 BPM]

[KEY: D minor]

[VOCALS: Intimate male vocal, Spoken-word rap verses with conversational phrasing, dry comic timing, and sharp pauses, Soft indie-folk sung hook with fragile warmth, Delivery starts sardonic and slightly defensive, then becomes more open and human, Close-mic verses, slight rasp on punchlines, occasional whispered doubles, Final section more melodic and less guarded, ]

[PRODUCTION: Fingerpicked acoustic guitar with a percussive, busker-like groove, Lo-fi hip-hop drums with soft kick, brushed snare, and hand taps, Warm upright-style bass, Sparse piano enters after the second hook, Subtle room noise for intimacy, Live, imperfect, close mix, Full-stop dropouts before punchlines, Final third adds soft harmonies and warmer bass, staying raw and uncluttered, ]
[LYRICS]
[Intro – spoken | sparse guitar]

Please reed carefully.

By continuing this conversation
you agree
that I may require
additional patience.

Side effects include:
overexplaining,
underspeaking,
leaving early,
arriving strange,
and needing three business days
to answer a simple question.

[Verse 1 – spoken rap]

They said, “You seem normal enough.”
Which is always a trap with curtains.

Like normal is a contract
and I forgot to check the wording.

Clause one:
I may nod like I understand
while my brain is still in the lobby
looking for the entrance.

Clause two:
I may laugh three seconds late
at a joke I did enjoy
but had to assemble manually
from tone, eyebrow, context,
and blind panic.

Clause three:
If the room gets loud
I reserve the right
to become furniture.

Not rude furniture.
Just a chair with survival instincts.

They say society is built for everyone.

Small print says:
everyone who can stand the lights,
read the room,
catch the hint,
keep the job,
make the call,
take the joke,
hold the gaze,
and not ask why the rules changed
between Tuesday and lunch.

[Pre-Hook]

I tried to sign my name
where the line was meant to be
But every page had footnotes
that sounded just like me

[Hook]

Terms and conditions apply
Some assembly required inside
Handle with care, but don’t ask why
I come with exceptions attached

Terms and conditions apply
I’m not broken, just hard to classify
Read the warning before you decide
I come with exceptions attached

[Post-Hook – spoken aside]

There’s a helpline.

It’s me.

I don’t answer.

[Verse 2 – spoken rap]

They love a clean little slogan
for a messy little world.

“Work hard.”
“Speak up.”
“Calm down.”
“Be yourself.”

Lovely.

Be myself, but filtered.
Speak up, but pleasant.
Calm down, but immediately.
Work hard, but invisibly.
Need help, but not too loudly.
Struggle, but make it inspiring.

There’s a whole economy
built on pretending the stairs are fair
because technically
there is a ramp
round the back
through a locked gate
beside a bin
with a sign that says
“Ask staff for assistance.”

Staff are busy.

Staff have attended a workshop
called “Empathy in Modern Environments”
and now say things like
“we hear you”
while changing nothing
with excellent posture.

Everyone has needs.
Everyone has limits.
Everyone has something.

But some of us arrive
with a terms booklet
thick enough
to level a table.

And the world keeps saying,
“Could you summarize that
in one sentence?”

Sure.

I am expensive
in ways you cannot invoice.

[Bridge – beat drops | vulnerable]

And yes—

sometimes I’m difficult.

Not mysterious.
Not poetic.
Just difficult.

Sometimes the plan changes
and I become weather.

Sometimes a text message
weighs more than furniture.

Sometimes the smallest errand
stands at the end of a hallway
holding a knife.

Sometimes I cancel
because my face is tired
from pretending
it knows what faces are for.

Sometimes I need silence
the way other people need air.

And yes, I know.

That is inconvenient.

Imagine living there.

[Verse 3 – warmer]

But I am not a failed version
of someone easier.

I am not a public service announcement
with shoes.

I am not your lesson
in patience
unless you are paying tuition.

I am a person
with strange weather,
careful exits,
unusual maps,
and a heart
that has spent years
learning how not to spill itself
on people who only came
to check the label.

So here are the revised terms:

I will try
to say what I need
before I disappear.

You will try
not to call every need
a negotiation.

I will try
to laugh before the moment dies.

You will try
to notice
I was laughing with you
even if I missed my cue.

I will try
not to turn every room
into a courtroom.

You will try
not to make every difference
stand trial.

And somewhere
between access
and apology,
between patience
and pride,

maybe we stop pretending
fair means identical.

Maybe we build the table
with the wobble in mind.

[Outro – spoken]

By staying this long
you accept
that understanding
may not be immediate.

Refunds are unavailable.

But tea may be provided.

And honestly?

That’s more than most systems offer.
