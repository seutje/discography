[TITLE: The Curator's Hands]
[GENRE: Alternative Hip-Hop / Acoustic Folk / Spoken Word]

[MOOD: Wry, reflective, uneasy, self-interrogating → tender, accountable, quietly resolved]

[TEMPO: 84 BPM]

[KEY: D minor]

[VOCALS: Intimate male vocal, Spoken-word rap with conversational timing, dry humor, and careful pauses, Soft sung refrains, slightly fragile but warm, Starts detached and museum-guide ironic, then grows more personal, Close-mic delivery, occasional whispered doubles on key phrases, Final section more melodic and human, ]

[PRODUCTION: Fingerpicked acoustic guitar with subtle percussive taps, Lo-fi drums, brushed snare, soft kick, warm bass, Sparse piano and muted strings enter halfway, Gentle room noise, chair creaks, breath kept in, Occasional dropouts before key lines, Arrangement feels like a late-night gallery tour becoming a confession, ]
[LYRICS]
[Intro – spoken | sparse guitar]

Welcome back.

Please keep your hands
away from the glass.

Unless, of course,
they’re mine.

In which case—

that explains
most of the fingerprints.

[Section I – The Gallery]

I used to call myself curator
like that made it clean
Like arranging the wreckage
was different from making the scene

A thousand little fragments
all polished and framed
Tiny borrowed torches
all speaking one name

I’d stand in the middle
with a clipboard and grin
Like, “None of this is chaos
if I organize it thin”

Here’s the longing wing
Here’s the father display
Here’s the childhood exhibit
Please don’t lean that way

Here’s a small angry room
where the comments still live
Here’s the joke I keep telling
so I don’t have to forgive

And here, behind glass,
is the part I call art:

a nervous little animal
pretending to be smart.

[Refrain – softly sung]

Oh, Curator
what did you keep?

What did you bury
to make the room look deep?

Oh, Curator
who did you become?

When every thousand voices
had to answer as one?

[Section II – The Label]

See, the label said
“mixed media, personal history”

which is a polite way of saying
“please don’t ask directly.”

The label said
“late period self-arrangement”

which is a polite way of saying
“I was scared of the basement.”

The label said
“themes of distance, class, and restraint”

which is a polite way of saying
“I wanted help and couldn’t complain.”

And people nodded.

Because labels are magic
when they’re printed small.

You can hide a whole collapse
on a white museum wall.

You can call it “material tension”
when the material is grief.

You can call it “negative space”
when what’s missing is belief.

You can call it “found object”
when the object is a child

left in too many rooms
and told to be mild.

[Refrain – warmer]

Oh, Curator
what did you move?

What did you soften
so the story felt smooth?

Oh, Curator
tell me the truth:

Did you protect the piece
or protect yourself from proof?

[Section III – The Log]

I found the old notes.

Not dramatic.

Just a folder
with dates that looked harmless.

Little records of attempts
to make the shape behave.

“Too bitter.”

“Too ornate.”

“Too direct.”

“Too much.”

“Not enough.”

“Try again, but make it sound
like someone survived it.”

That one hurt.

Because I did.

Sort of.

Depending how generously
you define survived.

I kept trying to make a person
out of evidence.

But every time I trimmed the edges
I lost the fingerprints.

Every time I made it balanced
it stopped breathing right.

Every time I made it pretty
it forgot the night.

There’s a difference
between care
and control.

I learned that late.

Which is on brand.

[Bridge – beat drops | spoken, bare]

Maybe I wasn’t making meaning.

Maybe I was making distance.

Maybe I kept the hard parts beautiful
so nobody would touch them.

Maybe I called it a gallery
because “storage unit for pain”
has terrible branding.

Maybe the earlier version of me
wasn’t wrong.

Just lonely
with better lighting.

[Section IV – The Hands]

So here’s what I know now.

The hands matter.

Not because they made everything
from nothing.

Nobody does.

We inherit rooms.
We borrow phrases.
We misremember weather.
We keep what cuts us
because at least it proves
something made contact.

The hands matter
because they choose.

This shard.
Not that one.

This silence.
Not yet.

This wound.
With a chair beside it.

This joke.
So the wound doesn’t think
it owns the house.

The hands matter
because they return.

Because they keep rearranging
what they couldn’t repair.

Because they lift the same piece
again and again
and whisper,

“Maybe not beautiful.

But honest there.”

[Final Refrain – sung, fuller but restrained]

Oh, Curator
open the room

Leave some dust
and leave some gloom

Oh, Curator
let it be touched

Not everything fragile
has to mean too much

Oh, Curator
lower the light

Let the crooked things
stay crooked tonight

If a thousand voices
still answer as one

maybe the name they speak
isn’t finished—

just begun.

[Outro – spoken | instruments fading]

So yes.

The fingerprints are mine.

The cracked frame too.

The missing label.

The overlong explanation.

The unnecessary metaphor
near the exit.

I’m not proud of every room.

But I know why each one’s there.

And for once,

I’m leaving the doors
unlocked.
