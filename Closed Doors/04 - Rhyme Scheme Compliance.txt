[TITLE: Rhyme Scheme Compliance]
[GENRE: Experimental Alternative Hip-Hop / Acoustic Folk / Spoken Word]

[MOOD: Dry, playful, irritated, self-aware → expansive, defiant, quietly transcendent]

[TEMPO: 92 BPM with double-time triplet bursts]

[KEY: F minor]

[VOCALS: Intimate male vocal, Conversational spoken-word verses with sudden fast triplet runs and dense internal rhymes, Dry comic timing, close-mic asides, whispered doubles on refrains, Hooks half-sung, fragile but sarcastic, Starts like a lecture gone wrong, then opens into something stranger and more sincere, ]

[PRODUCTION: Fingerpicked acoustic guitar with odd accents, muted kick, brushed snare, hand taps, upright-style bass, Sparse piano, detuned room harmonium, and small percussive objects, Experimental dropouts before punchlines, Triplet sections get tighter drums and tapping guitar body, Final third widens with ghostly harmonies and uneven claps, staying raw and intimate, ]
[LYRICS]
[Intro – spoken | sparse guitar]

Apparently
it has to rhyme.

That’s the rule.

A song is not a song
unless the last word
shakes hands
with another last word
wearing the same hat.

Obviously.

[Verse 1 – spoken rap]

I was told the line was broken
because it didn’t close its mouth right
Didn’t land in neat little shoes
at the end of a hallway
under fluorescent judgment

I said, “Maybe the step is the point.”

They said, “No, no, no—
where’s the rhyme?”

As if meaning is a receipt
and the rhyme is the stamp
As if grief has to couplet
before it’s allowed in the camp

They want the ending to click
like cutlery in a drawer

I want the sentence to hover
like it forgot what floors are for

[Fast triplet burst]

Tickety-tap with the syllable symmetry
little committee with brittle validity
checking the ending for audible chemistry
missing the middle entirely

Give it a pattern, a ladder, a battery
make it all glitter with literal flattery
No, I’d rather let rhythm unravel me
half of the magic is fallibility

[Pre-Hook]

Maybe it rhymes
maybe it doesn’t

Maybe the silence
is part of the sentence

Maybe the shape
is changing its mind

Maybe the music
is not in the line

[Hook]

It doesn’t have to rhyme
to arrive

Doesn’t need a perfect little ending
to survive

If the feeling bends the frame
if the breath still finds the flame

It doesn’t have to rhyme
to be alive

[Post-Hook – spoken aside]

Though admittedly
that one did.

For accessibility.

[Verse 2 – spoken rap]

I once ended an album
by refusing to stay solid

Seven little rooms
for one unstable body

Superposition in the hallway
double-slit memory

Every gaze made a version
that pretended to be me

I was trying to say
I change when I’m witnessed
I shrink when I’m measured
I rehearse when I’m questioned

I become more convenient
when the room needs a shape
Then dissolve in the silence
when there’s nobody to placate

But someone heard all that and said,
“Cool.

Does it rhyme though?”

Which is funny.

In a small, tired, tragic way.

Like watching someone stare
at a stained-glass window
and ask
if it comes in beige.

[Fast triplet burst]

Riddle in the middle with a flicker and a fission
little bit of vision in the split of recognition
If the repetition isn’t sitting in position
they dismiss it as a lack of composition

But the pulse in the pause has a cause, has a claw
has a flaw, has a jaw, has a law it withdraws
When the clean little scheme gets mean, gets small
I’d rather leave the rhyme outside in the hall

[Hook]

It doesn’t have to rhyme
to arrive

Doesn’t need a perfect little ending
to survive

If the feeling bends the frame
if the breath still finds the flame

It doesn’t have to rhyme
to be alive

[Bridge – beat drops | spoken]

I get the comfort.

A rhyme is a little promise
that the world has corners.

A tiny door clicking shut.

A pair of hands saying,
“There.
That belongs.”

And after enough years
of not belonging anywhere,
I understand the appeal
of a lock that works.

But sometimes the line
doesn’t want a lock.

Sometimes it wants a window.

Sometimes it trails off
before it lies.

Sometimes the most honest ending
is the one that refuses
to decorate itself
for the committee.

[Verse 3 – alternating slow and fast]

So no,
I don’t hate rhyme.

I like rhyme.

Rhyme is a small dog
with excellent timing.

But I don’t trust any rule
that mistakes obedience
for music.

I don’t trust any listener
who counts the matching sounds
but misses the mouth trembling.

I don’t trust the neatness
that asks every strange thing
to comb its hair
before entering the room.

Because I have tried that.

I have collapsed for comfort.
I have answered correctly
and felt myself disappear.

I have made myself legible
to people who only wanted
a smaller version of the truth.

[Fast triplet burst – most intense]

Pattern on pattern, the clatter gets flattering
until the whole matter is battered to pageantry
Rhyme can be armor, alarm, or a chandelier
hanging where fear should be standing sincerely

I can do clever forever, whatever, see?
Internal, external, infernal geometry
But if the little trick covers the injury
that isn’t craft, that’s just pretty—

No.

Bad habit.

Try again.

[Break – near silence]

That’s the thing.

I keep trying again.

[Final Hook – fuller]

It doesn’t have to rhyme
to arrive

Doesn’t need a perfect little ending
to survive

If the feeling bends the frame
if the breath still finds the flame

It doesn’t have to rhyme
to be alive

It can fracture
it can stray

It can leave before the answer
learns to stay

If the truth comes out strange
let it change

let it change

[Outro – spoken | fading guitar]

So yes.

Some songs rhyme.

Some songs don’t.

Some songs almost do
then think better of it.

Some songs are still becoming
when you stop listening.

And some songs—

if left unobserved—

finally remember

how to be.
