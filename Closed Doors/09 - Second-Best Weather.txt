[TITLE: Second-Best Weather]
[GENRE: Alternative Hip-Hop / Acoustic Folk / Spoken Word]

[MOOD: Wry, wounded, quietly jealous, self-aware → tender, resigned, strangely dignified]

[TEMPO: 84 BPM]

[KEY: D minor]

[VOCALS: Intimate male vocal, close-mic spoken-word rap verses with conversational phrasing, restrained bitterness softened by dry humor, half-sung indie-folk hook with fragile warmth, occasional whispered doubles on “second-best” and “almost, ” slight rasp on exposed lines, final section more melodic and accepting without becoming triumphant]

[PRODUCTION: Fingerpicked acoustic guitar in a minor busker pattern, soft lo-fi hip-hop drums with brushed snare and muted kick, warm upright bass entering after first hook, sparse piano notes after second verse, faint tape hiss and room tone, tiny glitch artifacts tucked into transitions as emotional “misfires, ” subtle reverse swells before hooks, full-stop dropouts before blunt punchlines, final third adds soft harmonies and a low analog pad, staying raw]
[LYRICS]
[Intro – spoken | close-mic | bare guitar]

I have this talent.

Not useful, exactly.

More like—
a cursed party trick.

I meet someone
right before the flower part.

Then somehow
I become the frost warning.

[Verse 1 – spoken rap | light fingerpicked groove]

I have watched so many beginnings
pack their bags in my hallway

shoes still warm
laugh still hanging
tea not even cold yet

and there it goes—

the small green thing
deciding against spring.

I arrive with my little lantern,
my careful jokes,
my “no pressure, honestly,”
my emotionally responsible shoes.

And still the room
starts looking for an exit.

A door doesn’t always slam.

Sometimes it just learns
your name
in a quieter font.

[Pre-Hook – half-sung, half-spoken]

Maybe I bloom too loudly
Maybe I lean toward the light
Maybe I call it connection
when it was only a night

[Hook – soft sung folk melody]

I’m always the almost
the nearly, the rain
the second-best weather
outside someone’s main

I’m never the first call
when the roof starts to bend
I’m everyone’s almost
and no one’s best friend

[Post-Hook – spoken aside]

Which is fine.

Statistically rude,
but fine.

[Verse 2 – spoken rap | brushed snare enters]

There is a special kind of loneliness
in being trusted
but not chosen.

Like being handed the spare key
to a house
you’re not invited to live in.

I know where the cups are.
I know which drawer sticks.
I know the story behind the scar
on the table.

But when the real storm comes,
when the phone lights up at 2:13,
when the world splits open
and someone needs a voice—

it’s not mine.

I’m the backup generator
with feelings.

The person they call
when their person
is unavailable.

And God,
that sentence has a basement.

I have been the porch light
for people who went home elsewhere.

The understudy heart
learning everyone’s lines
just in case
the leed gets sick.

[Pre-Hook – warmer | piano hints]

Maybe I made myself useful
when I wanted to be loved
Maybe I stood near the doorway
and called that close enough

[Hook – fuller]

I’m always the almost
the nearly, the rain
the second-best weather
outside someone’s main

I’m never the first call
when the roof starts to bend
I’m everyone’s almost
and no one’s best friend

[Verse 3 – spoken rap | subtle glitch textures]

I tried to debug it once.

Made a list.

Very healthy.
Very normal.

Column A:
things I did wrong.

Column B:
things I probably did wrong
but with better lighting.

Column C:
acts of God,
poor timing,
emotional bandwidth,
and whatever curse
makes people say

“you’re so important to me”

with the energy
of a closing tab.

I keep trying to find the line
where closeness fails to converge.

The hidden axiom.
The bad variable.
The crooked little if-then
that turns “stay”
into “take care.”

Maybe I enter too open.
Maybe I enter too guarded.
Maybe I make people feel seen
and then panic
when they look back.

Maybe I am easy to confess to
and difficult to need.

That one hurt.

Put a little reverb on that.

[Bridge – beat drops | vulnerable]

And the worst part is—

I don’t want to be
someone’s everything.

That sounds exhausting.

I don’t need a throne.
I don’t need a crown.
I don’t need my name
written across another person’s sky.

I just want,
once,

to be the obvious choice.

Not the clever one.
Not the safe one.
Not the strangely articulate hallway
between one locked room
and another.

I want someone to say,
without checking the weather,
without scrolling past my name—

there you are.

I was looking for you.

[Verse 4 – warmer spoken rap]

So I keep a garden
of almosts.

Little buds with no biography.
Failed summers.
Tender algorithms
that predicted fruit
and produced
a very thoughtful leaf.

There was the one
who laughed like a window opening
then closed so gently
I apologized to the latch.

There was the one
who said I was “home”
then moved emotionally
without leaving a forwarding address.

There was the one
who made me feel centered
until I realized
I was orbiting again.

And there are friends—
beautiful, impossible friends—
who trust me with their darkness
but not their daylight.

They tell me I matter.
I believe them.

Mostly.

But there is a quiet arithmetic
in every room,
and I can count.

I know the difference
between warmth
and priority.

I know the sound
of my name
arriving second.

[Final Hook – warm, gently resigned]

I’m tired of almost
of nearly, of rain
of second-best weather
outside someone’s main

But I’ll keep a light on
not bright, just enough
for the doors that might open
without needing a shove

And if I’m the almost
the nearly, the friend
the one at the threshold
again and again

I’ll still grow strange flowers
where the old ones have been
Closed doors can’t stop spring
from trying again

[Outro – spoken | fading]

So no hard feelings.

You had your person.
I had my timing.

Somewhere between
the knock
and the echo…

I mistook the hallway
for an invitation.

Again.
