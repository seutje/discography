[TITLE: Oars From Strangers]
[GENRE: Alternative Hip-Hop / Acoustic Folk / Spoken Word]
[MOOD: Wry, tired, salt-bitten, unexpectedly warm, gently uplifting]
[TEMPO: 86 BPM]
[KEY: D minor lifting toward F major in the final hook]
[VOCALS: Intimate close-mic spoken-word verses with dry timing, Half-sung folk hook, Small laugh kept in before the second verse, Warmer group harmonies in the final hook, Vocal stays human and unpolished]
[PRODUCTION: Fingerpicked acoustic guitar with percussive busker groove, Soft lo-fi drums with brushed snare and palm taps, Warm upright bass after first hook, Sparse piano in the bridge, Room tone and wooden creaks, Dropouts before punchlines, Final third opens with soft harmonies while staying raw]
[LYRICS]
[Intro - spoken | bare guitar]
Here is a thing
they do not put
in survival manuals:

Sometimes help arrives
looking badly organised.

One glove.
Three opinions.
A boat hook
held the wrong way round.

Beautiful, honestly.

[Verse 1 - spoken rap]
I had been rowing
with one useful thought
and one plank
that made accusations.

The sea kept giving me
employee feedback
in buckets.

Needs improvement.
Poor teamwork.
Unsuitable footwear.

Then through the morning
came a little skiff
with two old fishers
and a child eating pears.

No trumpets.
No grand rescue posture.

Just a woman with a scarf
saying,
"You look like a man
who has argued with a wave
and lost the minutes."

Which was specific.

And fair.

[Pre-Hook - half-sung]
I had learned to call the empty
my only honest crew
Then someone tossed an oar across
and said,
"We row this stretch too"

[Hook - soft sung folk melody]
Pass the oar, pull together
Not every mercy needs good weather
Hand by hand, we cross the swell
No one has to row it well

Pass the oar, mind the splinter
Share the bread, forgive the winter
If the boat keeps moving through
That is enough for me and you

[Post-Hook - spoken aside]
Also,
the child offered me
half a pear
with the solemn confidence
of a tiny dockmaster.

I accepted.

Obviously.

[Verse 2 - spoken rap | drums enter]
They did not ask
for my whole sad inventory.

No clipboard.
No moral weather report.

Just,
"Can you sit up?"
"Can you drink?"
"Do you know where you are?"

And I said,
"Mostly."

Because honesty
is important,
but panic
does not need extra admin.

The old man laughed
like a rope coming loose.

He said,
"Nobody knows where they are
after a bad crossing.
You learn by rowing
next to someone
for a while."

Then he pointed at the leak
with professional disappointment
and handed me a cup.

Great.

Group bailing.

[Pre-Hook - warmer]
I thought I had to earn the deck
by never needing room
But kindness came with wet boots on
and made a little room

[Hook - fuller]
Pass the oar, pull together
Not every mercy needs good weather
Hand by hand, we cross the swell
No one has to row it well

Pass the oar, mind the splinter
Share the bread, forgive the winter
If the boat keeps moving through
That is enough for me and you

[Bridge - spoken | piano enters]
I thought of the spare key
to houses I never lived in.

I thought of second-best weather
standing outside the main event
with damp socks
and a useful face.

At sea,
useful is not a punishment.

At sea,
the person with the cup
keeps everyone afloat.

[Final Hook - communal, warm]
Pass the oar, pull together
Not every mercy needs good weather
Hand by hand, we cross the swell
No one has to row it well

Pass the oar, mind the splinter
Share the bread, forgive the winter
If the boat keeps moving through
That is enough for me and you

[Outro - spoken]
By dusk,
my raft was tied
behind their skiff,

still ugly,
still mine,

but no longer
the whole ocean.
