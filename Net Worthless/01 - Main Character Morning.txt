[TITLE: Main Character Morning]
[GENRE: Alternative Hip-Hop / Acoustic Folk / Spoken Word]

[MOOD: Sardonic, irritated, observant, comic, socially exhausted → softer, wiser, quietly hopeful]

[TEMPO: 88 BPM]

[KEY: D minor]

[VOCALS: Intimate male vocal, Spoken-word rap verses with conversational pacing, clipped pauses, dry punchline timing, half-sung folk hook, Starts bitter and amused, gradually turns reflective, Close-mic delivery, slight rasp on sarcastic lines, whispered doubles on key phrases, Final hook warmer and more melodic]

[PRODUCTION: Fingerpicked acoustic guitar with percussive street-corner groove, Lo-fi hip-hop drums: soft kick, brushed snare, palm taps, Warm upright-style bass, Sparse piano after second hook, Light room hiss for intimacy, Live and imperfect, sharp dropouts before punchlines, Final third adds soft harmonies and fuller bass while staying raw and uncluttered]
[LYRICS]
[Intro – spoken | dry, close-mic | sparse guitar]

There’s a man at the traffic light
staring into his phone
like God just messaged him
“u up?”

The light is green.

Behind him, twelve cars
are becoming monks.

One woman whispers,
“Go.”

He doesn’t.

Because clearly
the city has paused
for his thumb.

[Verse 1 – spoken rap | light guitar groove]

I’ve met the stars of tiny films
shot entirely in their heads
They walk through crowds like parted seas
except nobody has moved yet

Headphones in
face set to brave
like every pavement is a stage
and we’re all background actors
with no right to complain

There’s a bloke on the left
of the escalator
standing like a monument
to poor behaviour

You say, “Excuse me.”
He sighs like a saint
as if stepping aside
is a moral complaint

And I get it, mate
life is hard
you’ve got a tote bag
and emotional scars

But some of us have trains
and knees
and rage
and a meeting that started
three minutes ago

Stand on the right
walk on the left
It’s not a poem
It’s just respect

[Hook – soft sung folk melody]

Everybody’s starring
in a film no one bought tickets for
Main character morning
with your bag across the corridor

Look up for a second
There are people in the frame
The world doesn’t end
when it isn’t saying your name

[Post-Hook – spoken aside]

Controversial.
Might lose followers.

[Verse 2 – spoken rap | drums enter]

There’s a lift at the office
doors slide wide
and before we can leave
they’re already inside

Like salmon upstream
but with perfume
marching straight into
a two-metre room

I’m holding a laptop
a coffee
a coat
and the last thin thread
of a civic oath

They push through smiling
like, “Sorry, just me,”
which is exactly the problem
philosophically

You can’t enter a space
before space has been made
That’s not etiquette
That’s primary grade

Green means go
doors mean wait
pavements are shared
and queues have a shape

But lately it feels
like “I need” became law
and “sorry” means
“I’m doing it more”

[Hook – fuller, warmer]

Everybody’s starring
in a film no one bought tickets for
Main character morning
with your bag across the corridor

Look up for a second
There are people in the frame
The world doesn’t end
when it isn’t saying your name

[Bridge – beat drops | spoken, anxious]

And here’s the ugly bit.

I’m scared I’m one of them.

Not sometimes.
Often.

I check my phone
then hate phone people.

I block an aisle
while judging the aisle-blockers.

I take up space
with my bag
my mood
my unresolved childhood
and a pastry.

I build a courtroom
in my skull
and appoint myself judge
because it’s easier
than admitting
I’m terrified
of being careless.

Terrified that my kindness
is just manners
with good lighting.

Terrified that I only notice selfishness
when it inconveniences me.

Maybe this whole song
isn’t a sermon.

Maybe it’s a panic attack
with a guitar.

Maybe I point at strangers
because I’m begging myself:

Don’t become that.
Don’t become that.
Don’t become that.

[Verse 3 – warmer spoken rap | piano enters lightly]

Because most people aren’t evil.

They’re late.
They’re sad.
They’re under-slept.
They’ve got rent in their chest
and a message they regret.

They’re carrying fathers
blood tests
breakups
and lunches
left on the counter.

They’re not thinking,
“How can I ruin this stranger’s morning?”

They’re thinking,
“Please let me survive mine.”

And still—

Move right on the escalator.

Let people out of the lift.

Green means go.

A doorway is not
a place to discover
your inner weather.

There’s a quiet contract
written nowhere
signed by everyone
in tiny daily choices

Hold the door
make room
look up
step aside

Small mercies
are not small
when everyone is drowning
inside

[Final Hook – warm, gently defiant]

Everybody’s starring
in a film no one bought tickets for
Main character morning
with your bag across the corridor

Look up for a second
There are people in the frame
The world doesn’t end
when it isn’t saying your name

Everybody’s carrying
a world behind their eyes
But the kindest little miracle
is making room in mine

So move with the river
Let the tired ones through
The world doesn’t spin
only for you

[Outro – spoken, slight smile | instruments fading]

The light’s still green, by the way.

He looks up.

Finally drives.

Fourteen people forgive him
in different languages
using only their horns.

And somewhere
on an escalator
a man stands right.

Not because he’s perfect.

Because for one small second
he remembered—

other people
are also
trying to get somewhere.
