[TITLE: Sadness With a Tax]
[GENRE: Alternative Hip-Hop / Acoustic Folk / Spoken Word]

[MOOD: Witty, weary, socially observant, emotionally broke → vulnerable, tender, quietly defiant]

[TEMPO: 86 BPM]

[KEY: D minor]

[VOCALS: Intimate male close-mic vocal, spoken-word rap verses with dry comic timing and clipped pauses, half-sung folk hook with fragile warmth, starts sardonic and guarded, gradually cracks open, slight rasp on punchlines, whispered doubles on key phrases, final hook warmer and more melodic]

[PRODUCTION: Fingerpicked acoustic guitar with percussive groove, lo-fi hip-hop drums, brushed snare, palm taps, warm upright bass, sparse piano after second hook, room hiss, live imperfect feel, dropouts before punchlines, final third adds soft harmonies and fuller bass while staying raw]
[LYRICS]
[Intro – spoken | dry, close-mic]

I checked my emotional balance this morning.

Insufficient funds.

Which is rude,
because I distinctly remember
having a life.

A couch.
A plant.
Three mugs
with different levels of personality.

Technically,
I’m thriving.

Terms and conditions apply.

[Verse 1 – spoken rap]

Rent went up,
bread went up,
gas went up,
my little public smile
got hit with a service charge.

I’m in the supermarket
doing maths with my soul—
can I afford joy,
or just something joy-adjacent
with a damaged box?

I used to feel things wholesale:
wonder in bulk,
sadness by the kilo,
hope with no receipt.

Now every ache
has a handling fee,
and grief comes wrapped
in plastic security tape.

I’m window-shopping happiness
behind glass.

The tag says:
“peace of mind”
reduced from
“being loved properly.”

Still too much.

So I cut coupons of meaning
from old conversations.

Buy one purpose,
get one crisis free.

[Hook – sung folk melody]

I’m still alive,
just priced out of feeling,
smiling in installments
while the ceiling keeps peeling.

Window-shopping joy
like it’s locked behind glass,
can’t afford the present
so I joke about the past.

If sadness had a tax,
I’d be owing by the minute.
I’m bankrupt in the chest,
but I’m still living in it.

[Verse 2 – spoken rap]

Everyone’s broke,
but nobody dresses like it.

Fake Gucci smiles
on clearance-bin faces,
walking through town
like sponsored replacements.

They say,
“How are you?”
with premium teeth.

I say,
“Can’t complain,”
which is basically fraud.

Because I can complain.

At length.

With supporting documents.

But who can afford honesty
in this economy?

Who’s got the budget
for a full breakdown
with heating on?

So we all perform fine
under fluorescent lights,
laughing like rent
isn’t living in our lungs.

I keep importing hope
with extra tariffs.

Customs holds it for weeks
then charges me storage.

By the time it arrives,
it’s smaller than advertised.

Some assembly required.
No batteries.
No returns.

[Hook – fuller]

I’m still alive,
just priced out of feeling,
smiling in installments
while the ceiling keeps peeling.

Window-shopping joy
like it’s locked behind glass,
can’t afford the present
so I joke about the past.

If sadness had a tax,
I’d be owing by the minute.
I’m bankrupt in the chest,
but I’m still living in it.

[Bridge – spoken | vulnerable]

I miss being sad
for free.

I miss crying
without checking
if I had the time.

Now falling apart
feels like a luxury subscription
with a free trial
I forgot to cancel.

I schedule despair
between laundry
and emails,
then hit snooze

because I’m tired
from pretending
I’m economically viable
as a person.

Sometimes I think
the world doesn’t want us happy.

It just wants us functional.

Low battery.
High brightness.

Still scrolling.

[Verse 3 – warmer]

But yesterday
I saw a man
give half his sandwich
to a stranger.

No camera.
No caption.

Just bread
moving from one hunger
to another.

And for a second
my chest remembered
it was not a bank.

It was a room.

Messy.
Bad wiring.
One window painted shut.

But still a room.

Maybe meaning
isn’t something we buy.

Maybe it’s the receipt
we keep finding
in our pockets—

proof that somewhere,
somehow,
we paid attention.

[Final Hook – warm]

We’re still alive,
just priced out of feeling,
smiling in installments
while the ceiling keeps peeling.

Window-shopping joy,
but the glass starts to crack.
Can’t afford the future,
but we’re walking toward it back.

If sadness has a tax,
let them send me the letter.
I’m bankrupt in the chest,
but I’m getting there better.

[Outro – spoken]

So yes.

I’m fine.

But not in the way
they mean it.

Fine like a coat
with one button left.

Still here.

Still technically a life.

Still checking the reduced section
for something
that looks enough like hope
to take home.
