[TITLE: Minimum Wage Messiah]
[GENRE: Alternative Hip-Hop / Acoustic Folk / Spoken Word]

[MOOD: Witty, wounded, class-conscious, tender → quietly furious, humane, stubbornly hopeful]

[TEMPO: 86 BPM]

[KEY: D minor]

[VOCALS: Intimate male close-mic vocal, spoken-word rap verses with dry comic timing and clipped pauses, half-sung folk hook, starts observant and sardonic, grows warmer and more direct, slight rasp on punchlines, whispered doubles on key phrases, final hook more melodic and communal]

[PRODUCTION: Fingerpicked acoustic guitar with percussive busker groove, lo-fi hip-hop drums, brushed snare, palm taps, warm upright bass, sparse piano after second hook, room hiss, live imperfect feel, hard dropouts before punchlines, final third adds soft harmonies while staying raw]
[LYRICS]
[Intro – spoken | dry, close-mic | sparse guitar]

There’s a man outside the shop
with three coins,
two carrier bags,
and a cigarette
he keeps forgetting to smoke.

Someone asks him for change.

He laughs.

Not because it’s funny.

Because the till
inside his chest
already says no.

Then he gives them
one of the coins.

Some people call that poor judgement.

I call it theology.

[Verse 1 – spoken rap | light guitar groove]

Minimum wage messiah
with holes in his trainers,
turning water into tap water
and still sharing later.

He’s got rent in arrears,
bread reduced after eight,
a phone with one percent
and a cracked little faith.

But he’ll split a sandwich
like a sacred transaction,
half for his hunger,
half for compassion.

No press.
No plaque.
No grateful committee.
Just kindness
with a receipt too thin
to claim back from the city.

Meanwhile a man
with seven spare houses
says tax is a burden
from one of his couches.

Says wealth needs freedom.
Says markets need air.
Says people should try
being born with a chair.

And somewhere in town
a cleaner counts pennies
to buy someone tea
who has fewer than any.

Tell me again
who the economy blesses.

Tell me again
who keeps failing the test.

[Hook – soft sung folk melody]

Minimum wage messiah,
empty pockets, open hands,
giving away nothing
like it’s loaves upon the sand.

Those with everything
keep asking what it costs,
while the ones with almost nothing
pay the debt for all we’ve lost.

If mercy had a payslip,
they’d be rich beyond belief.
But the kindest little prophets
still can’t afford relief.

[Verse 2 – spoken rap | drums enter]

I’ve seen saints
in supermarket uniforms,
name tags crooked,
souls outworn.

Mothers at tills
with feet full of thunder,
scanning cheap pasta
and swallowing hunger.

They round down prices
when nobody sees,
slip extra napkins
beside weak coffees.

They say,
“Don’t worry, love,”
like it isn’t expensive
to sound that gentle
while life gets offensive.

And the rich?

The rich have opinions
about other people’s fridges.

They say,
“Work harder.”
They say,
“Spend less.”
From a lunch
that could cover
three months of stress.

They call tax theft
with a straight little face,
like survival is greedy
and hoarding is grace.

They’ve got offshore islands
for feelings and funds,
little sunny accounts
where accountability runs.

But ask them to pay
so a kid can eat dinner—

suddenly the mansion
gets spiritually thinner.

[Hook – fuller, warmer]

Minimum wage messiah,
empty pockets, open hands,
giving away nothing
like it’s loaves upon the sand.

Those with everything
keep asking what it costs,
while the ones with almost nothing
pay the debt for all we’ve lost.

If mercy had a payslip,
they’d be rich beyond belief.
But the kindest little prophets
still can’t afford relief.

[Bridge – beat drops | spoken, vulnerable]

And I know
poverty isn’t holy.

Let’s not dress pain up
and call it a lesson.

Nobody should have to become generous
because the system left them
nothing else to give.

There is no romance
in an empty meter.

No moral wisdom
in choosing between heating
and dinner.

But there is something
almost impossible

about a person
counting their last coins

then looking at another person
and thinking:

You first.

That should shame
every golden tower
in the skyline.

That should rattle
every locked drawer
where the surplus sleeps.

That should make
the comfortable
feel audited.

[Verse 3 – warmer spoken rap | piano enters lightly]

Maybe holiness
isn’t thunder.

Maybe it’s bus fare
handed over
by someone walking home.

Maybe it’s a coat
on a colder back.

A tea paid forward.

A tired nurse
smuggling a joke
through a twelve-hour shift.

Maybe grace
is the minimum payment
made by people
already drowning in interest.

Maybe love
is the only currency
that gets bigger
when spent.

And maybe one day
we stop praising the poor
for surviving the wound

and start billing the people
who sold us the knife.

Until then,
there’s a man by the shop
with two coins now,
one carrier bag,
and no cigarette.

Still laughing.

Still broke.

Still richer
than the men
who could save the world

and ask
for a deduction.

[Final Hook – warm, gently defiant]

Minimum wage messiah,
empty pockets, open hands,
giving away nothing
like it’s loaves upon the sand.

Those with everything
keep asking what it costs,
while the ones with almost nothing
pay the debt for all we’ve lost.

If mercy had a payslip,
they’d be rich beyond belief.
But the kindest little prophets
still can’t afford relief.

Minimum wage messiah,
walking home beneath the rain,
gave away his nothing
and called it someone’s gain.

[Outro – spoken | slight smile | instruments fading]

The till says no.

The heart says
try anyway.

Somewhere,
a billionaire
is explaining fairness
to a chandelier.

Somewhere else,
a hungry man
breaks bread

and the whole broken world

almost
balances.
