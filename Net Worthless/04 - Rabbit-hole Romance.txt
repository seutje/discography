[TITLE: Rabbit-hole Romance]
[GENRE: Alternative Hip-Hop / Acoustic Folk / Spoken Word]

[MOOD: Witty, anxious, vulnerable, self-aware → wounded, baffled → quietly defiant]

[TEMPO: 86 BPM with brief double-time spoken bursts]

[KEY: D minor, lifting toward F major in final hook]

[VOCALS: Intimate male vocal, close-mic spoken-word rap verses with dry comic timing, sudden rhythmic overthinking bursts, soft fragile sung folk hooks, whispered doubles on key phrases, final section warmer and more melodic]

[PRODUCTION: Fingerpicked acoustic guitar, lo-fi brushed drums, hand taps, warm upright bass, sparse piano after verse two, tiny magic-show details: card flicks, coin rolls, stage creaks, dropouts before punchlines, raw close mix with room tone and soft final harmonies]
[LYRICS]
[Intro – spoken | bare guitar]

For my next trick

I will attempt
to become lovable

while following
the instructions

I was not given.

Please watch closely.

Apparently
that helps.

[Verse 1 – spoken rap]

I arrived with flowers
but not too many flowers

because too many flowers says
“future shed.”

I arrived with questions
but not interview questions

because nothing kills romance
like a man with follow-ups
and a moral spreadsheet.

I said,
“You look lovely,”

then panicked
because lovely felt old-fashioned

so I added,
“in a very current way,”

which helped no one.

I remembered eye contact,
then not too much eye contact,

then forgot where eyes go
when they are off duty.

I laughed at the joke.

Correct volume.
Acceptable duration.
No visible subtitles.

Honestly,
there should have been applause.

[Pre-Hook – half-sung]

I had the cards
I had the dove

I had the line
I had the cue

I just never had
the trick explained to me

[Hook – soft sung]

Rabbit-hole romance
watch me disappear

You said the night was magic
then you left me standing here

No trapdoor
no reveal

no little note to say what’s real

Just a hat on the table
and the shape of where you were

Rabbit-hole romance
I still don’t know the turn

[Post-Hook – spoken aside]

Which is fine.

I have only checked
seventeen possible reasons.

This morning.

[Verse 2 – spoken rap]

Dating apps are talent shows
for people with lighting.

Everyone says,
“Just be yourself,”

then immediately adds
filters.

Be honest,
but breezy.

Be keen,
but hydrated.

Be available,
but not like a shop.

Be funny,
but don’t make the wound
do all the catering.

So I built a profile
like a tiny museum
of acceptable strangeness.

One joke.
One dog-adjacent photo.
One sentence implying depth
without causing paperwork.

I wrote,
“I like knowing exactly
what time we are leaving.”

Deleted it.

Too honest.

I wrote,
“Ask me about my special interests.”

Then imagined
a stranger’s thumb
hovering above my entire personality

like a judge
with excellent nails.

[Fast spoken burst]

Tick the box for stable,
tick the box for charming,

tick the box for “not too much
at half past morning.”

Mask it with a grin,
pack it in a tin,

say the right thing
with the wrong face again.

Every small confession
needs a risk assessment.

Love is apparently
a group presentation.

[Hook – fuller]

Rabbit-hole romance
watch me disappear

You said the night was magic
then you left me standing here

No trapdoor
no reveal

no little note to say what’s real

Just a hat on the table
and the shape of where you were

Rabbit-hole romance
I still don’t know the turn

[Bridge – beat drops | spoken]

And the worst part is—

I could take rejection.

Probably.

With water.
And a chair.
And three to five working days.

What breaks me
is the vanishing.

The little puff of smoke
where a reason should be.

Because then I become
detective,
suspect,
jury,
janitor,

and the rabbit.

Was it the joke
about trains?

Was I too eager?
Too careful?
Too exact?
Too soft?
Too much?

Somewhere between
the first drink
and “let’s do this again”

there must be
a small mechanical click

where I stopped being possible.

[Verse 3 – warmer spoken rap]

I’ve been the third body
at tables built for two,

drifting through perfect little orbits
with my coat still on.

I’ve been second-best weather,

the useful kind of rain
outside someone else’s summer.

I’ve been the almost,
the nearly,

the man people trust
with their unlit rooms

but not their Sunday morning.

And still—

I keep bringing a lantern.

A brave little idiot flame
with shoes.

Because beneath the edited message,
the rehearsed hello,

the hand held still
so it doesn’t flap its biography
across the table,

there is a person.

Not a puzzle box.

Not a trick.

Not a lesson in patience
with cheekbones.

A person
who wants to be chosen

before he has to provide
a glossary.

[Break – near silence | spoken]

Watch closely.

This is the part
where I stop
sawing myself in half

to prove
I can fit inside
the cabinet.

[Final Hook – warm, defiant]

Rabbit-hole romance
I may disappear

But not every missing answer
gets to keep me living here

No trapdoor
no reveal

no final proof to make it heal

Just my hands on the table
and the truth I didn’t burn

Rabbit-hole romance
I still don’t know the turn

Rabbit-hole romance
maybe that’s the thing

Some people leave the theatre
before the heart learns how to sing

No white glove
no grand design

no perfect reason I can find

If they won’t show the trick
I won’t call myself the error
in the turn

[Outro – spoken | fading guitar]

For my final trick

I will not text again.

I will not write
a twelve-part theory

with citations.

I will stand here.

Awkwardly.

Humanly.

Rabbit in one hand.
Flowers in the other.

And when the lights come up,

I will bow

without apologizing
for the magic
they walked out on.
