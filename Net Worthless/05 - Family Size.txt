[TITLE: Family Size]
[GENRE: Alternative Hip-Hop / Acoustic Folk / Spoken Word]

[MOOD: Wry, anxious, self-mocking, vulnerable → tender, defiant, quietly hopeful]

[TEMPO: 86 BPM]

[KEY: A minor, lifting toward C major in final hook]

[VOCALS: Intimate male vocal, Spoken-word rap verses with conversational phrasing, sharp pauses, dry comic timing, Soft indie-folk sung hook, Starts sardonic and nervous, becomes exposed and human, Close-mic verses, slight rasp on confession lines, occasional whispered doubles, Final section warmer and more melodic]

[PRODUCTION: Fingerpicked acoustic guitar with percussive busker groove, Lo-fi hip-hop drums: soft kick, brushed snare, hand taps, Warm upright-style bass, Sparse piano after second hook, Gentle organ pad in final third, Live, imperfect, close mix, Full-stop dropouts before punchlines, Final hook adds soft harmonies while staying raw and uncluttered]
[LYRICS]
[Intro – spoken | dry, close-mic | sparse guitar]
Funny thing.

The packet says
“new look.”

Same price.

Less inside.

Which is rude.

Because lately
I’ve been looking
suspiciously
new look myself.

[Verse 1 – spoken rap | light guitar groove]
I picked up a chocolate bar
from the corner shop shelf
and thought,
“Hang on, mate.
You used to be yourself.”

Same wrapper.
Same smile.
Same confident font.
Same little promise
of exactly what I want.

But the thing in my hand
felt thin as an excuse
like joy had been audited
for casual use.

And I laughed.

Then I didn’t.

Because that’s been me lately.

Same face in the glass
but the contents feel shady.

Same name.
Same coat.
Same jokes on repeat.

But I walk up the stairs
and negotiate with my feet.

I open a book
I once would have swallowed
and now page three
has a hill to follow.

I used to catch thoughts
like coins from the air.

Now I pat down my pockets
and nothing is there.

[Pre-Hook – half-sung, half-spoken]
Maybe I’m tired
Maybe I’m scared

Maybe the shelf looks the same
but there’s less of me there

[Hook – soft sung folk melody]
Am I family size
with the family gone?

Same little box
with the old weight wrong

They kept the price
but they took out the shine

Is there a floor to falling
or is falling the design?

[Post-Hook – spoken aside]
Also, who decided
twenty-seven crisps
was a serving?

I’d like names.

[Verse 2 – spoken rap | brushed snare enters]
There was a time I could juggle
six tabs in my brain

Two conversations
and a kettle of pain

A deadline.
A bill.
A half-written plan.

Now I lose my keys
while they’re still in my hand.

I read the same sentence
nine times in a row

like maybe the tenth
will agree to come home.

I stare at a form
with my government face

and suddenly language
has left the place.

Things that were easy
now ask for a chair.

Emails arrive
and I need medical care.

A password reset
feels mythic and cruel.

A printer says “error”
and I leave the room.

And people say,
“That’s normal.
You’re just getting older.”

Which is comforting
in the way
a wet sock
is a blanket.

Because I don’t just miss youth.

I miss ease.

I miss opening the door
and trusting the keys.

I miss being quick
without making a speech.

I miss feeling the answer
just slightly in reach.

[Hook – fuller, warmer]
Am I family size
with the family gone?

Same little box
with the old weight wrong

They kept the price
but they took out the shine

Is there a floor to falling
or is falling the design?

[Bridge – beat drops | spoken, vulnerable]
And I know.

Everyone says
decline is natural.

Which is true.

So is mildew.

Doesn’t mean
I want it
in the corners
of my mind.

I’m scared there’s no bottom.

That’s the bit.

Not that I’m slower.

Not that my back now makes noises
like an old ship
considering prayer.

Not that I need recovery time
from sitting badly.

It’s the wondering.

Is this a dip?

A season?

A warning?

A receipt?

Is my brain tired
or leaving by degrees?

Did someone quietly reduce
the grams of me

while keeping
the packaging neat?

And what if I adapt
to this smaller version

then wake up next year
smaller again?

How many losses
can you call adjustment

before the word
starts looking away?

[Verse 3 – spoken rap | piano enters lightly]
I used to think ageing
would arrive with a bell

A dramatic little cloak
and a story to tell

But it came as small thefts
in sensible shoes

A name I misplaced
A bruise I didn’t choose

A night out requiring
three days of repair

A sock on the floor
becoming a dare

A sharp little word
from someone I love

because my patience ran out
with the milk and the gloves

And yes, I still laugh.

I’m not made of glass.

I can still make a joke
while fearing the maths.

Because humour’s the trolley
with the squeaking wheel

that carries the dread
to somewhere less real.

But beneath all the bits
and the shop-floor complaint

there’s a kid in my chest
saying,
“Tell me I ain’t

being replaced
by a cheaper design

with smaller ideas
and a slower mind.”

[Pre-Hook – soft, almost sung]
Maybe I’m less
than I was yesterday

Or maybe I’m measuring wrong
on a terrible day

[Final Hook – warm, gently defiant]
Am I family size
with the family gone?

Same little box
with the old weight wrong

They kept the price
but they took out the shine

Is there a floor to falling
or is falling the design?

Maybe I’m smaller
Maybe that’s true

But smaller things still hold
what they’re meant to

A match is small
but it knows how to glow

A seed looks finished
till it starts to grow

[Outro – spoken | instruments softening]
So yes.

Maybe there’s less in the packet.

Maybe the label lies.

Maybe I need more rest now.

More lists.

More kindness.

Less pretending
I’m a warehouse
with unlimited stock.

But I am not empty.

Not yet.

There’s still something here
worth opening.

Still salt at the bottom.
Still crumbs in the corner.

Still enough sweetness
to make a tired person
stand in the kitchen

at midnight

and say,
"Alright then.
One more day"
