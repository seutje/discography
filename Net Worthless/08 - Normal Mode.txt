[TITLE: Normal Mode]
[GENRE: Alternative Hip-Hop / Acoustic Folk / Spoken Word]

[MOOD: Witty, anxious, overstimulated, self-mocking → vulnerable, tender, quietly defiant]

[TEMPO: 86 BPM]

[KEY: D minor, lifting toward F major in the final hook]

[VOCALS: Intimate male close-mic vocal; spoken-word rap verses with conversational pacing, clipped pauses, dry punchline timing, occasional fast overthinking bursts; soft half-sung folk hook; whispered doubles on “normal mode, ” “too much, ” and “let me be”; final hook warmer and more communal]

[PRODUCTION: Fingerpicked acoustic guitar with percussive busker groove; lo-fi hip-hop drums with soft kick, brushed snare, hand taps; warm upright bass; room hiss; small sensory details tucked low in the mix: kettle click, fluorescent buzz, phone vibration, cutlery clink; hard dropouts before punchlines; sparse piano after second hook; final third adds soft harmonies and fuller bass while staying raw]
[LYRICS]
[Intro – spoken | dry, close-mic | sparse guitar]

I woke up this morning
and selected
normal mode.

Optimistic,

because normal mode
requires an update

and my brain
is running on
three percent,

two biscuits,

and a sound
nobody else
can hear.

[Verse 1 – spoken rap | light guitar groove]

I walked into the shop
with a list in my hand

like a paper priest
sent to make demands.

Milk.
Bread.
Soap.
Something green

so the fridge looks less
like a cry for help.

The lights were bright
in a personal way.

The freezer hummed
like it knew my name.

A child screamed once
near the frozen peas

and my soul left quietly
through aisle three.

I smiled at the cashier.

Correct face.
Good tone.

No visible evidence
I was buffering alone.

They said,
“Need a bag, mate?”

I said,
“No thanks, I’m fine,”

holding seven loose items

and the concept
of decline.

Because fine
is a costume
with an elastic waist.

Wear it all day
if you don’t breathe
at pace.

[Pre-Hook – half-sung]

I know the lines
I know the scene

I know where the person
is meant to be

But under the skin
where the wires glow blue

there’s a little red light
saying,
not for you

[Hook – soft sung folk melody]

I’m trying to run
in normal mode

smiling while the system
overloads

Too much light
too much room

too many tabs
in a one-bed moon

If I go quiet
don’t call it rude

I’m finding a door
through the noise and the queue

I’m still here
I’m still me

just not built
for pretending cheaply

[Post-Hook – spoken aside]

Also,
who decided
background music
needed ambition?

I’m buying onions,
not entering a boss fight,
cheers.

[Verse 2 – spoken rap | drums enter]

At work they love
a casual meeting.

Business for
“surprise social eating.”

Someone says,
“Let’s brainstorm,”

and suddenly my thoughts
form a union
and leave.

I nod like a bloke
with available bandwidth.

Inside, I’m filing
for emergency silence.

Spreadsheet open,
Teams call blinking,

three people laughing
while I’m manually thinking:

Eye contact.
Not too much.

Laugh here.
Cup touch.

Question.
Answer.
Don’t explain the machine.

Keep the strange bits
off the screen.

[Fast spoken burst]

Mask in the morning,
mask in the lift,

mask with coffee
and a socially approved quip,

mask when the printer
makes that noise,

mask when the joke
has invisible decoys,

mask till the jaw
forgets its hinge,

mask till the self
comes loose at the fringe.

Then someone says,
“You seem so calm,”

and I nearly applaud
with my actual palm.

[Hook – fuller, warmer]

I’m trying to run
in normal mode

smiling while the system
overloads

Too much light
too much room

too many tabs
in a one-bed moon

If I go quiet
don’t call it rude

I’m finding a door
through the noise and the queue

I’m still here
I’m still me

just not built
for pretending cheaply

[Bridge – beat drops | spoken, vulnerable]

And the ugly bit is—

sometimes I don’t know
where the mask ends.

I come home
and peel off my face

then find another face
politely waiting.

I cancel plans
I wanted.

I answer late
because the message
became furniture

and now it lives there.

I love people.

That’s the problem.

I love them
with a battery
that dies
in public.

And I’m tired
of calling myself difficult

when I mean
accurate.

Tired of saying
“sorry, I’m weird”

when I mean
the room is loud,

the tag is sharp,

the rules are smoke,

and I’ve been translating
all day

without a dictionary.

[Verse 3 – warmer spoken rap | piano enters lightly]

Maybe I’m not broken.

Maybe I’m specific.

A radio tuned sideways
still catches the weather.

A busy map
can still get you home.

A nervous system
is not a moral failure.

It’s a body
with strong opinions
about lighting.

Let me leave early
without making it tragic.

Let me ask twice
without calling it panic.

Let me eat the same safe tea
for a week

and still contain
mystery.

Let me stim
with my thumb
on my sleeve.

Let me need what I need
and not perform a receipt.

There’s kindness
in saying,

“This is too much.”

There’s courage
in the sentence,

“I can’t do that today.”

There’s a whole small life
on the other side

of not sanding yourself
into something
easy to display.

[Final Hook – warm, communal]

We’re trying to run
in normal mode

smiling while the system
overloads

Too much light
too much room

too many tabs
in a one-bed moon

If we go quiet
don’t call it rude

we’re finding a door
through the noise and the queue

We’re still here
still complete

not made wrong,
just made precisely

So dim the room
let the knackered ones breathe

leave space in the plan
for the need to leave

The world won’t end
if the script comes loose

Some hearts speak better
with the volume reduced

[Outro – spoken | slight smile | instruments fading]

So yes.

I’m fine.

But not
in the factory setting.

I will not become smaller
to make the room
feel clever.

I will not apologise
for hearing the buzz.

I am here.

Unmuted,
eventually.

And somewhere
under the table,

my foot is tapping
a tiny hymn

that got me
through the day.
