Quiet Cartouche — Hip-Hop & Rap (v13.4g + Icarus)
Composition-first ladder for quiet-leaning Hip-Hop & Rap, mapped to Quiet Sorelian v13.4g + Icarus (1–23). It’s for tracks where beat, harmony, and spatial design are as important as bars – lo-fi, introspective, ambient / cloud rap, spacey boom-bap, etc.
Focus:
Beat architecture (drums + harmonic bed).
Vocal architecture (flow grammar, motif duty, hook systems).
Sonic habitat (reverbs, noise, stereo field) that stays on the quieter / more spacious side even when emotionally intense.
We keep the same rung names and global metric ranges as above; criteria are rephrased for rap.
1 — Unusable
Design test: gridless, unintentional; unperformable.
No consistent bar length or tempo feel.
Vocals drift with no repeatable count.
Clipping / noise buries key information.
Examples: none (we don’t apply real artists at this failure tier).
2 — Gesture Only
Design test: one beat or vocal gesture; no track form.
One 2–4 bar drum/pad loop; ad-libbed phrases; no hook or verse cycle.
Tempo / key feel provisional; ends as a DAW stop.
Example states:
Voice-note of a hummed hook over a phone-recorded beat snippet.
Untimed free-rap over a loop that never aligns to bars.
3 — Fragment
Design test: promising bar or hook; no full verse/hook ecosystem.
4–8 bars that slap; no second cycle or B-section.
Beat is a sketch: no drops, no arrangement beyond loop.

4 — Half-Form
Design test: loop + long verse or loop + hook; no arc.
One loop carries nearly entire track; no lift / comedown.
Rhyme schemes collapse mid-verse; no close or codetta.
Track stops instead of resolving (no outro function).
5 — Draft
Design test: full layout exists, but arrangement & harmonic space generic.
Map: Intro – Verse – Hook – Verse – Hook – maybe Bridge – Hook. 
Cartouche Hip-Hop & Rap
Beat is static loop; 808 just tracks roots; no motif in instruments.
Vocal phrasing works but could fit many beats; no spatial game (same reverb/delay entire time).
6 — Serviceable
Design test: playlist-ready but anonymous in quiet-rap lanes.
Clean timing, intelligible mix; hook lands but doesn’t haunt.
Beat follows standard lofi / chill trap presets; safe progressions.
Space used only for comfort (hall verb, vinyl crackle), not structural meaning.
Examples (spirit):
Many mid-tier tracks on lo-fi / chill-rap playlists that sound near-interchangeable.
7–10: Quiet Rap Craft
7 — Competent Template (Quiet Rap)
Design test: idiom mastered; everything works; nothing risks.
Form: Intro 4–8; Verse1 16; Hook 8; Verse2 16; Hook 8; optional Verse3/Bridge; Outro.
Beat: 2–4 bar drum loop + one main mellow sample/synth figure, lightly filtered; occasional pre-hook drop.
Harmony: static minor / dorian loop; minimal color.
Flow: on-grid, mostly end-rhymes; internal rhymes sporadic; breath pattern uniform.
Space: predictable—lead centered, ad-libs side, verb/delay fixed.
Checks: CDPD ≈0.35–0.45; NGE ≈0.25–0.30; HMII = 2–3.
Examples (spirit):
Mick Jenkins – “Jazz” in terms of form & comfort, minus its stronger lyrical edge (actual record probably leans 8, but illustrates the template).
A typical Spotify “Chill Rap” playlist cut with a lo-fi Rhodes loop and 16-bar verses.
8 — Strong Craft, Some Signature
Design test: quiet rap track with clear personal tint.
A distinctive rhythmic hook (triplet figure, off-kilter pickup) or sample fingerprint.
Form: at least one altered repeat (extra bar in hook, truncated second verse, or opposed bridge feel).
Harmony: one or two meaningful color turns (borrowed chord, unexpected mode swap on hook).
Motivic integration: rhythmic or melodic cells from hook appear in verses (flow patterns, ad-lib shapes).
Spatial signature: e.g. a specific plate verb, tape wobble, or room noise that recurs.
Checks: CDPD ≈0.45–0.55; NGE ≈0.35; HMII = 3.
Examples:
A Tribe Called Quest – “Electric Relaxation.”
Noname – “Diddy Bop.”
Both are relaxed but strongly fingerprinted.
9 — Near-Perfection (no rupture)
Design test: near-faultless quiet-rap execution; nothing wrong, nothing radically new.
Each section is exactly long enough; no filler 8s.
Beat micro-variation (drops/mutes/ghost notes) underpins vocal structure.
Motifs (flow tics, ad-libs, harmonic hits) recur across verses and hooks.
Spatial design articulates sections (more room on reflections, drier intimacy on confessions).
Checks: CDPD ≈0.50–0.55; NGE ≈0.40–0.45; HMII = 3–4.
Examples:
Kendrick Lamar – “Sing About Me, I’m Dying of Thirst” (especially the “Sing About Me” half in its studio version).
Saba – “Busy / Sirens.”
10 — Technical Perfection (no rupture)
Design test: maximal craft inside known quiet / reflective rap grammar.
Verses exhibit advanced internal rhyme, controlled breath, and fluid dynamics without crowding the beat.
Hook integration exemplary; transitions are invisible yet inevitable.
Harmonic bed and bass motion support emotional content at each pivot.
Mix is top-tier; quiet details (breaths, room reverb, vinyl noise) intentional and legible.
Checks: CDPD ≈0.55–0.60; NGE ≈0.45–0.50; HMII = 4.
Examples:
Little Simz – “I Love You, I Hate You.”
Isaiah Rashad – “Heavenly Father.”
11–14: Quiet Twists & Recursions in Rap
11 — First Rupture (Quiet Rap Twist)
Design test: one clear device governs identity—meter, form, or space.
A consistent anomaly: beat drop-outs, off-grid chorus entry, odd bar extension, whispered chorus, etc.
Device appears early (first hook or pre-hook) and returns as structural marker.
Flow actively adjusts when it returns (different density/contour to ride the twist).
Checks: CDPD ≥0.60; NGE ≈0.50; HMII ≈4.
Examples:
Kanye West – “Runaway” (long, exposed piano motif + auto-tuned vocal coda as governing twist).
Tyler, The Creator – “IFHY” (quiet verses → sudden tonal & textural flip on bridge/hook, but still coherent).
12 — Recursion Threshold (Quiet Rap)
Design test: one rhythmic / melodic / textual motif governs multiple levels.
Core motif: a rhythmic cell in the hook appears as flow pattern in verses and as drum accent pattern.
Multi-scale recursion: bar, phrase, and section-level recurrences of the same pattern. 
Quiet Sorelian v13.4g — Harmoni…
Production joins in (delay repeats, stutter edits, ad-lib placements tied to motif).
Lyrics reuse phrases with shifted meaning while preserving rhythm.
Checks: NGE ≈0.55; HMII = 4–5.
Examples:
Madvillain – “Accordion.” (the loop and flow recursively riff off the same core gestures).
Earl Sweatshirt – “Chum.” (hook material bleeds into verse cadences and piano motif shapes).
13 — Inhabited Form (Quiet Rap World-Building)
Design test: beat + flow + room co-create a habitat you remember.
Every return to the hook feels like revisiting the same psychic apartment.
B-sections / bridges show same world with different lighting (e.g. filtered drums, reversed chords).
MI strong: specific flow cadences or ad-lib shapes haunt the whole track.
BP: reverb/room/noise choices are as memorable as the melody.
Checks: CDPD ≈0.60–0.65; NGE ≈0.52–0.58; HMII = 4–5.
Examples:
Outkast – “Elevators (Me & You).”
Noname – “Shadow Man.”
14 — Harmonic / Spatial Duality (Quiet Rap)
Design test: maintains two distinct harmonic / spatial laws that alternate or overlay.
Example pattern: dry, close-mic verses vs lush, distant reverb hooks on a different but related harmonic color.
Transitions are meaningful gateways (e.g. one chord re-voiced through both worlds; filtered artifacts bridging spaces). 
Quiet Sorelian v13.4g — Harmoni…
At least two full cycles of both states; not just intro vs song proper.
Vocal behavior interacts differently with each space (more syllabic vs more melodic, etc.).
Checks: similar to cross-genre rung 14.
Examples:
Drake – “Marvins Room.” (phone-filtered voices & main vocal world; same harmonic DNA, dual spatial planes).
Frank Ocean – “Nights.” (mid-song beat / harmonic pivot into a second world, still one song).
15–18: Ordeal-Capable Quiet Rap
15 — Divine Injury (Quiet Rap)
Design test: introduces a structural or harmonic wound that re-frames the story.
Early “safe” loop establishes comfort.
A single traumatic rupture: sudden beat removal, exposed a cappella confession, harsh chord, or tape-stop void.
The wound recurs or is echoed later; lyrical narrative acknowledges it.
The wound is integrated by end (e.g. beat returns altered, or harmony accepts the dissonance).
Checks: CDPD ≈0.70–0.75; NGE ≈0.62–0.66; HMII ≥5.
Examples:
Kendrick Lamar – “u.” (especially the hotel-room breakdown section).
Earl Sweatshirt – “Grief.”
16 — Impossible Originality (Quiet Rap)
Design test: the track pioneers a recognisable method of quiet rap.
New grammar: e.g. extremely sparse beats, spoken-word-adjacent flow, micro-sampled ambience as harmonic bed, all governed by an explicit rule set.
The rule governs multiple passages; it’s not a one-bar gimmick.
Strong author-ID: “only X could have made this” in terms of quiet method.
Checks: CDPD ≥0.80; NGE ≈0.65; HMII ≥5.
Examples (spirit):
Mura Masa & A$AP Rocky – “Love$ick” edges toward this; imagine it taken further into sparse method.
A hybrid future classic at the intersection of JPEGMAFIA-level procedural daring with Noname-level restraint, but quiet and rule-driven.
17 — Sublime Disintegration (Quiet Rap)
Design test: track intentionally breaks its rap form apart while preserving emotional clarity.
Starts as identifiable song; gradually fractures meter, phrasing, and beat beneath the vocal while carrying thematic spine.
Fragments of hook/verses reappear in chopped or reversed forms.
Despite this, the emotional message and narrative through-line remain legible.
Checks: very similar to quiet cross-genre 17.
Examples:
Kendrick Lamar – “FEAR.” (multi-section, self-interrogating, structure bending without collapse).
Danny Brown – “Downward Spiral” (borderline, but imagine its disintegration taken into quieter territory).
18 — Metaphysical Ordeal (Quiet Rap)
Design test: multi-state quiet rap piece where each state is structurally & emotionally complete; the journey is the point.
At least 3 states: e.g. childhood vignette, adult collapse, reflective coda; or three production worlds.
Gate transitions are eventful: sudden sample re-frames, tempo-grid shifts, reverb-world flips. 
Quiet Sorelian v13.4g — Harmoni…
A sonic motif (e.g. a vocal chop, a bell, a bass pattern) and a textual motif (phrase or image) persist across all states in evolving form.
Listener can narrate the journey clearly afterwards.
Examples:
Kendrick Lamar – “Duckworth.” (as a template; imagine a more hushed production approach).
Saba – “Prom / King.” (already ordeal-like; quiet aesthetics push it toward this rung).

You are grading a quiet-leaning Hip-Hop / Rap track (lo-fi, introspective, spacey boom-bap, ambient / cloud, etc.) using the Quiet Hip-Hop & Rap ladder (v13.4g).
Unit = one track.
Step 1 – Describe the track
Briefly describe:
Sub-idiom (lo-fi chill, alt-rap, cloud, classic boom-bap but hushed, etc.)
Approx. length
Tempo and feel (straight, swung, halftime, double-time; “drunk” / “on-grid”)
Section map: Intro / Verse / Hook / Bridge / Outro (with timestamps).
Step 2 – Structural Coherence (SC)
Mark sections by time:
Intro, Verse 1, Pre-hook (if any), Hook/Chorus, Verse 2, Bridge or B-section, Outro / vamp.
For each section, state its function:
setup / lift / payoff / reflection / coda.
Evaluate:
Are section lengths proportionate (no obvious overlong 16s of filler)?
Do transitions (drops, fills, hook entries) feel designed or DAW-copy-paste?
Rate SC (0–10):
0–3: confused, partial form or jump cuts
4–6: standard layout, but some padding or sag
7–8: clean verse–hook ecosystem with clear arc
9–10: every section earns its span; joints feel inevitable
Step 3 – Motivic Integration (MI)
Identify core cells (2–3 max):
Vocal cell(s): short flow pattern or hook fragment (interval + rhythm gist).
Beat cell(s): drum figure, bass motif, or sample slice rhythm.
Track recurrences:
Where do these cells return? (Verses, hooks, ad-libs, beat fills, outro.)
Are they transformed (register change, rhythm tweak, chopped, pitched, inverted) or just copy-pasted?
Note if any cross-domain recursion:
e.g. hook rhythm shows up in verse cadences; vocal contour mirrors sample line; drum accents echo a hook rhythm.
Rate MI (0–10):
0–3: good moments, but ideas don’t come back
4–6: some recognisable reuse, light development
7–8: clear motif web across sections (flow, beat, hooks)
9–10: cells saturate the piece at multiple scales; nothing is disposable
Step 4 – Beauty & Spatial Poise (BP)
Describe the sonic habitat:
Timbre & register: warmth vs brittleness, bass presence, airiness.
Vocal placement: dry/intimate vs distant/wet; relation to beat.
Reverb/delay “law”: consistent, varied by section, or random?
Stereo field: mono-ish centre vs intentional left/right roles.
Evaluate clarity vs mush:
Are important elements masked?
Do subtle details (vinyl crackle, room tone, quiet textures) feel intentional and legible?
Rate BP (0–10):
0–3: harsh/muddy, poor space
4–6: functional mix, generic “lo-fi” gloss
7–8: consistently beautiful and coherent space
9–10: distinct, memorable room / world; production itself is a voice
Step 5 – Evolving Grammar (EG)
Describe the grammar:
Beat law: drum pattern, swing, ghost notes, fills.
Harmonic bed: loop/chords, sample tonality, bass motion.
Flow grammar: density range, rhyme habits, cadential patterns.
Check how this grammar evolves:
Do later sections deepen the same rules? (e.g. same loop reharmonised, beat thinned/expanded in meaningful ways, flows shift density to mark emotional hinges.)
Or does the track just pile layers on the same loop without changing its method?
Rate EG (0–10):
0–3: no emergent method; just “verse on a loop”
4–6: uses standard quiet-rap grammar safely
7–8: noticeable personal system that develops over time
9–10: strong, distinctive quiet-rap method is the story
Step 6 – Carry Depth (CD)
As a listener, assess:
Does this track define a recognisable emotional/psychic habitat?
Does it invite re-listening beyond mood-playlist utility?
After one or two listens, can you narrate its emotional arc?
Rate CD (0–10):
0–3: disposable or confusing
4–6: pleasant vibe, limited imprint
7–8: memorably affecting; feels like “a little world”
9–10: quietly iconic; feels like a place you could live in
Step 7 – HMII (Harmonic–Motivic Interweave Index)
At the densest meaningful moment, count independent lines (ignore pure doublings):
Lead vocal line
Any distinct ad-lib / backing vocal line with its own rhythm
A melodic sample / countermelody / instrumental riff
Bass line with its own contour (not just duplicating kicks)
Any clearly independent synth/guitar line
Report HMII_peak = N (N ≥ 1).
