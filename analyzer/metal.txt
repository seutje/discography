Progressive Rock / Progressive Metal / Djent Cartouche
Sorelian v13.4g + Icarus Adapter

Unit = one track.

Use this method for:
- Progressive rock
- Progressive metal
- Djent
- Instrumental prog
- Technical / rhythmic metal
- Atmospheric prog-metal
- Hybrid prog tracks with clean/heavy contrast

Core focus:
- Sectional architecture: riffs, themes, movements, transitions, climaxes.
- Rhythmic grammar: odd meters, polymeter, displacement, syncopation, groove logic.
- Motivic integration: riffs, rhythmic cells, melodic themes, drum motifs, vocal hooks.
- Sonic weight and spatial poise: guitar tone, low-end control, drum clarity, ambience, density.
- Evolving grammar: whether the track’s rules develop over time instead of merely stacking riffs.
- Carry depth: whether the track feels like a journey, world, ordeal, or merely a technical display.

Main axes:
SC = Structural Coherence
MI = Motivic Integration
BP = Beauty / Brutality / Spatial Poise
EG = Evolving Grammar
CD = Carry Depth

Core metrics:
CDPD = Continuity-Derived Pattern Depth
How much the track’s riffs, sections, transitions, and climaxes feel derived from its own internal continuity rather than genre templates.

NGE = Novel Grammar Extension
How much new musical grammar the track introduces: rhythmic laws, tuning systems, form logic, riff syntax, tone design, orchestration, or hybrid language.

HMII = Harmonic–Motivic Interweave Index
At the densest meaningful moment, count independent musical lines:
- Lead vocal or lead melody
- Main guitar riff
- Second guitar counter-riff / harmony
- Bass line with independent contour
- Drum pattern with independent rhythmic logic
- Keyboard / synth / orchestral layer
- Ambient / noise / texture layer with structural function
- Backing vocals / choir / growls / clean counterline

Do not count pure doublings.
Report HMII_peak = N.

RUNG BANDS

1–6 = Sub-craft / pre-template
7–10 = Strong craft inside known prog / metal / djent grammar
11–14 = First new-grammar behaviors
15–18 = Ordeal-capable prog architecture
19–20 = Forbidden / event-level innovation
21–23 = Icarus multi-axis apex

--------------------------------------------
RUNG LADDER
--------------------------------------------

1 — Unusable
Design test:
The track fails basic musical function.
No stable tempo, tuning, riff identity, section logic, or intentional chaos.
Performance or production problems make the piece unlistenable.

2 — Gesture Only
Design test:
One riff, texture, drum groove, or harmonic idea exists, but no full track.
It feels like a rehearsal fragment, tone test, or DAW loop.

3 — Fragment
Design test:
A promising riff, meter idea, melody, or atmosphere appears, but there is no beginning-to-end form.
No meaningful development, transition system, or resolution.

4 — Half-Form
Design test:
At least two sections exist, but the track lacks architectural glue.
Common signs:
- Riffs are pasted together without transition logic.
- Heavy / clean contrast exists but does not evolve.
- Odd meters appear but do not serve the form.
- Ending feels like a stop, not a resolution.

5 — Draft
Design test:
A full map exists, but it is generic or underdeveloped.
Typical map:
Intro – Riff A – Verse – Chorus – Riff B – Solo / Break – Chorus – Outro.
Problems:
- Riffs do not transform.
- Bass mostly follows guitar roots.
- Drums support instead of converse.
- Production is serviceable but not release-level.
- The track has parts, but not a journey.

6 — Serviceable
Design test:
The track works and is listenable, but anonymous.
Traits:
- Clean execution.
- Familiar prog-metal / djent moves.
- Competent transitions.
- Predictable heavy-soft dynamics.
- Memorable only as “a decent track in the style.”

--------------------------------------------
7–10: PROG / DJENT CRAFT
--------------------------------------------

7 — Competent Template
Design test:
The idiom is mastered, but nothing risks identity.

Form:
Intro riff, verse riff, chorus / theme, breakdown, solo or bridge, final chorus / reprise.

Rhythm:
Odd meters or syncopation may appear, but they are standard genre vocabulary.
Djent patterns rely on familiar low-string chugs, accents, and subdivisions.

Harmony:
Minor / modal / Phrygian / Lydian colors are used safely.
Clean sections use predictable suspended or add9 chords.

MI:
Riffs may return, but mostly as repetition, not transformation.

BP:
Guitar tone, drums, bass, and ambience are functional but conventional.

Checks:
CDPD ≈ 0.35–0.45
NGE ≈ 0.25–0.30
HMII = 2–3

Examples in spirit:
A well-produced but generic modern prog-metal / djent track with tight guitars, clean choruses, and a breakdown that does exactly what the genre expects.

8 — Strong Craft, Some Signature
Design test:
The track has clear craft plus a recognizable fingerprint.

Markers:
- One memorable riff, rhythmic cell, vocal hook, or melodic theme.
- At least one altered repeat: shortened bar, extended phrase, metric turn, reharmonized chorus, or transformed breakdown.
- Clean/heavy contrast feels intentional, not formulaic.
- Drums and guitars converse rhythmically.
- Bass has occasional independent movement.
- Production has a recognizable tone or spatial identity.

Checks:
CDPD ≈ 0.45–0.55
NGE ≈ 0.35
HMII = 3

9 — Near-Perfection, No Rupture
Design test:
Near-flawless execution inside known prog / djent grammar.

Markers:
- Every section earns its length.
- No riff overstays.
- Transitions feel composed, not edited.
- Main motifs recur across riffs, vocals, solos, drums, or endings.
- Dynamic arc is clear: tension, escalation, release.
- Technical playing serves the song instead of showing off.
- Mix remains clear under density.

Checks:
CDPD ≈ 0.50–0.55
NGE ≈ 0.40–0.45
HMII = 3–4

10 — Technical Perfection, No Rupture
Design test:
Faultless execution of established progressive grammar.

Markers:
- Performances are exact but alive.
- Odd meters feel natural.
- Groove remains heavy even under complexity.
- Guitar/bass/drum lock is flawless.
- Clean and heavy tones are equally polished.
- Solos, fills, and transitions are structurally necessary.
- No detectable weak section, mix flaw, or filler passage.

Checks:
CDPD ≈ 0.55–0.60
NGE ≈ 0.45–0.50
HMII = 4

--------------------------------------------
11–14: NEW-GRAMMAR PROG BEHAVIORS
--------------------------------------------

11 — First Rupture
Design test:
One clear rule-bending device defines the track’s identity.

Possible devices:
- A recurring bar-length anomaly.
- A polymetric riff that becomes the track’s signature.
- A clean section that obeys a different harmonic law.
- A breakdown that arrives through melodic transformation rather than impact alone.
- A guitar tone, tuning, or production device that becomes structural.

Requirements:
- The twist appears early.
- The band adapts around it.
- It returns as a signpost.
- It occupies a meaningful part of the runtime, not just one bar.

Checks:
CDPD ≥ 0.60
NGE ≈ 0.50
HMII ≈ 4

12 — Recursion Threshold
Design test:
One musical cell generates multiple levels of the track.

Core cell may be:
- A rhythmic chug pattern.
- A melodic interval shape.
- A drum accent sequence.
- A vocal phrase rhythm.
- A harmonic movement.
- A pick-scrape / noise / texture gesture.

Multi-scale use:
- Micro: riff-level cell.
- Meso: phrase or section shape.
- Macro: whole-form layout or climax design.

Examples:
- A 5-note rhythmic cell becomes the guitar riff, vocal phrasing, drum accents, and section lengths.
- A clean arpeggio becomes the chorus melody, then returns as a distorted breakdown.

Checks:
NGE ≈ 0.55
HMII = 4–5

13 — Inhabited Form
Design test:
The track feels like a world, not just a sequence of riffs.

Markers:
- Guitar tone, drum room, bass weight, synths, ambience, and vocals belong to one habitat.
- Heavy sections and clean sections feel like different rooms in the same building.
- Reverbs, delays, distortion, and stereo width evolve with the form.
- Each instrument has a role: ground, blade, sky, pulse, narrator, shadow.
- Returning themes feel like returning to a location.

Checks:
CDPD ≈ 0.60–0.65
NGE ≈ 0.52–0.58
HMII = 4–5

14 — Harmonic / Rhythmic Duality
Design test:
The track sustains two independent tensions at once while remaining clear.

Possible dualities:
- 4/4 vocal line over polymetric guitars.
- Beautiful clean melody over destabilizing low-string rhythm.
- Major / Lydian upper harmony over dark bass movement.
- Drums imply one pulse while guitars imply another.
- Clean spacious world and tight mechanical world alternate or overlap.

Requirements:
- Duality lasts at least one full major section.
- Both layers are legible.
- The listener can still feel the song, not just admire the math.

Checks:
CDPD ≈ 0.70
NGE ≈ 0.60
HMII ≥ 5

--------------------------------------------
15–18: ORDEAL-CAPABLE PROG
--------------------------------------------

15 — Divine Injury
Design test:
One rupture permanently changes the track.

The wound may be:
- Sudden silence after density.
- A catastrophic tempo shift.
- A harsh dissonant chord.
- A collapse from full band to exposed clean guitar or voice.
- A breakdown that destroys the previous groove.
- A tonal shift the track never fully recovers from.

Requirements:
- The wound is foreshadowed.
- The exact moment is identifiable.
- Afterward, the track obeys changed rules.
- The ending carries the scar.

Checks:
CDPD ≈ 0.70–0.75
NGE ≈ 0.62–0.66
HMII ≥ 5

16 — Impossible Originality
Design test:
The track invents a recognizable method.

Possible methods:
- A new rhythmic grammar for riffs.
- A repeatable tuning / harmonic system.
- A unique clean-heavy transformation law.
- A distinctive production-performance method.
- A riff construction system only this band seems to use.

Requirements:
- The method governs at least 50% of the track.
- It could generate more works.
- The track still works emotionally and musically.
- An expert listener could say, “only this artist could have made this.”

Checks:
CDPD ≥ 0.80
NGE ≈ 0.65
HMII ≥ 5

17 — Sublime Disintegration
Design test:
The track intentionally breaks its own form apart without losing meaning.

Trajectory:
1. Stable identity.
2. Hairline cracks.
3. Fragmentation.
4. Reassembly, ghost return, or meaningful collapse.

Possible techniques:
- Riffs return out of phase.
- Themes appear in wrong registers or meters.
- Drums and guitars desynchronize then re-lock.
- Clean motifs are distorted beyond recognition.
- The chorus or main theme returns as a damaged artifact.

Requirement:
The emotional spine remains legible.

Checks:
CDPD ≈ 0.78–0.82
NGE ≈ 0.66–0.70
HMII ≥ 5

18 — Metaphysical Ordeal
Design test:
A multi-state progressive journey where each state is complete.

Requirements:
At least 3 major states, such as:
- Atmospheric invocation.
- Technical conflict.
- Crushing descent.
- Clean reflection.
- Transcendent reprise.

Each state must:
- Have its own internal grammar.
- Last long enough to matter.
- Transform shared motifs.
- Contribute to a narratable journey.

Checks:
NGE ≈ 0.68–0.70
HMII ≥ 5

--------------------------------------------
19–23: FORBIDDEN / ICARUS PROG
--------------------------------------------

19 — Forbidden Tone
Design test:
The track makes a normally “wrong” element central and beautiful.

Forbidden elements may include:
- Ugly guitar tone.
- Excessive mechanical tightness.
- Extreme tuning instability.
- Noise, clipping, feedback, pick scrape, amp hum.
- Inhuman grid precision.
- Near-silence inside metal.
- Anti-groove.
- Uncomfortable dissonance.

Requirement:
The forbidden element becomes the track’s emotional language without breaking musicality.

Checks:
CDPD ≥ 0.85
NGE ≥ 0.60
HMII ≥ 5

20 — Apocryphal Event Horizon
Design test:
The track introduces two independent innovations that co-carry the work.

Examples:
- A new rhythmic riff grammar plus a new spatial production method.
- A new tuning system plus a new long-form architecture.
- A new vocal/guitar relationship plus a new breakdown logic.
- A new approach to heaviness plus a new harmonic-motivic recursion system.

Requirements:
- Each innovation would still matter alone.
- Together they define new territory.
- Both operate across at least 50% of the runtime.
- No axis collapses: SC, MI, BP, EG, and CD all remain extremely high.

Checks:
CDPD ≥ 0.85
NGE ≥ 0.70
HMII ≥ 5

21 — Icarus 21
Header:
Icarus: 20×3 → IT=21

Design test:
Three axes among SC, MI, BP, EG, CD reach Tier-20 level across a sustained apex span.

Example:
A final movement where structure, motif, and emotional carry all peak simultaneously while the band remains fully clear and controlled.

22 — Icarus 22
Header:
Icarus: 20×4 → IT=22

Design test:
Four axes reach Tier-20 level simultaneously.

Example:
A long-form progressive metal suite where structure, motif, sonic beauty/brutality, and evolving grammar all reach apex without sacrificing emotional carry.

23 — Icarus 23
Header:
Icarus: 20×5 → IT=23

Design test:
All five axes reach Tier-20 level, plus a context-level innovation.

Possible context innovations:
- A new performance format.
- A generative or modular album form.
- A spatial / live setup that is part of the composition.
- A documented repeatable method that opens a new compositional field.

This is theoretical apex territory.

--------------------------------------------
TRACK-GRADING METHOD
--------------------------------------------

You are grading a progressive rock / progressive metal / djent track using the Prog / Djent Sorelian ladder.

Unit = one track.

Step 1 — Describe the track

Briefly describe:
- Sub-idiom:
  Progressive rock, progressive metal, djent, tech-metal, atmospheric prog, instrumental prog, etc.
- Approximate length.
- Tempo and feel:
  Straight, swung, halftime, double-time, odd-meter, polymetric, rubato intro, grid-tight, organic, etc.
- Tuning / tonal center if obvious:
  Standard, drop tuning, extended range, low F#, etc.
- Section map with timestamps:
  Intro / Riff A / Verse / Chorus / Clean break / Solo / Breakdown / Reprise / Outro.

Step 2 — Structural Coherence, SC

Mark sections by time:
- Intro
- Main riff / theme
- Verse or statement
- Chorus / refrain / melodic theme
- Bridge / clean section
- Solo / instrumental development
- Breakdown / heavy apex
- Reprise
- Outro / coda

For each section, state its function:
- Setup
- Tension
- Lift
- Contrast
- Development
- Payoff
- Collapse
- Reflection
- Coda

Evaluate:
- Are section lengths proportionate?
- Do odd meters and transitions feel composed or arbitrary?
- Does the track earn its long runtime?
- Are riffs connected by transformation, or simply pasted together?
- Does the ending resolve, rupture, or intentionally suspend?

Rate SC from 0–10:
0–3 = confused, incomplete, or pasted together
4–6 = standard layout with some padding or awkward joints
7–8 = strong arc; sections clearly function
9–10 = every section earns its span; transitions feel inevitable

Step 3 — Motivic Integration, MI

Identify 2–3 core cells:
- Guitar riff cell:
  rhythm, interval shape, picking pattern, accent pattern.
- Drum cell:
  kick/snare accent logic, cymbal pattern, displacement figure.
- Melodic / vocal cell:
  hook contour, chant rhythm, clean melody, scream cadence.
- Harmonic cell:
  chord color, progression, pedal tone, modal shift.
- Texture cell:
  ambient swell, noise gesture, synth pulse, feedback tone.

Track recurrences:
- Where do these cells return?
- Are they transformed or copied?
- Do they appear across instruments?
- Does a vocal rhythm become a guitar rhythm?
- Does a drum accent become a riff accent?
- Does a clean melody return as a heavy riff?

Rate MI from 0–10:
0–3 = good isolated riffs, little return
4–6 = some recurrence, mostly repetition
7–8 = clear web of riff/theme transformations
9–10 = cells saturate the whole piece at multiple scales

Step 4 — Beauty / Brutality / Spatial Poise, BP

Describe the sonic habitat:
- Guitar tone:
  tight, raw, saturated, glassy, fizzy, warm, mechanical, organic.
- Bass:
  audible independent voice or just low-end reinforcement?
- Drums:
  natural, programmed, hyper-edited, roomy, dry, explosive.
- Vocals:
  clean, harsh, layered, distant, intimate, theatrical, choral.
- Keys / synths / ambience:
  decorative or structural?
- Stereo and depth:
  wide guitars, centered rhythm section, spatial clean sections, immersive ambience.

Evaluate:
- Is heaviness clear or muddy?
- Does low-end remain controlled?
- Are dense sections legible?
- Are clean sections beautiful or just “the quiet part”?
- Does production articulate form?
- Does the mix create a memorable world?

Rate BP from 0–10:
0–3 = harsh, muddy, weak tone, unclear space
4–6 = functional modern metal / prog production
7–8 = strong tone-world; density and space controlled
9–10 = production is a compositional voice; beauty and brutality are both memorable

Step 5 — Evolving Grammar, EG

Describe the track’s grammar:
- Riff grammar:
  chugs, slides, tapping, syncopation, tremolo, chords, polymeter.
- Rhythm grammar:
  meter changes, displacement, subdivision games, groove cycles.
- Harmonic grammar:
  modal center, chromaticism, extended chords, pedal tones, key shifts.
- Arrangement grammar:
  how the track adds, removes, mutates, or recontextualizes material.
- Vocal grammar:
  clean/harsh contrast, phrasing, lyrical rhythm, call-response, layering.

Check evolution:
- Do later sections deepen the same rules?
- Are riffs transformed, compressed, expanded, reharmonized, or rhythmically displaced?
- Does the breakdown emerge from prior material?
- Does the solo develop themes or merely decorate?
- Does the track’s method become clearer over time?

Rate EG from 0–10:
0–3 = no emergent method; riffs just happen
4–6 = standard prog/djent grammar used safely
7–8 = noticeable personal system that develops
9–10 = the track’s evolving rule-system is the story

Step 6 — Carry Depth, CD

Assess the listener impact:
- Does the track feel like a journey?
- Does it invite re-listening beyond technical admiration?
- Can you narrate the emotional arc after one or two listens?
- Does the heavy material carry emotional meaning?
- Do the clean / atmospheric parts deepen the track rather than pause it?
- Does the ending leave a residue?

Rate CD from 0–10:
0–3 = disposable, confusing, or merely technical
4–6 = enjoyable but limited imprint
7–8 = memorable journey; strong emotional architecture
9–10 = iconic, inhabitable, ordeal-like, or transformative

Step 7 — HMII Peak

At the densest meaningful moment, count independent lines:

Possible lines:
1. Lead vocal / scream / clean melody
2. Main guitar riff
3. Second guitar harmony or counter-riff
4. Bass line with independent contour
5. Drum pattern with independent accent logic
6. Keyboard / synth / orchestral line
7. Ambient / noise / texture layer with structural function
8. Backing vocal / choir / counter-vocal
9. Lead guitar / solo line
10. Percussion / electronic pulse / secondary rhythm layer

Ignore pure doublings.

Report:
HMII_peak = __

Interpretation:
1–2 = sparse or simple
3–4 = normal dense prog/metal arrangement
5–6 = high interweave
7+ = extreme multi-layer architecture, only valid if still legible

Step 8 — Assign Sorelian Rung

Give:
- Rung: 1–23
- Band:
  1–6 = sub-craft / pre-template
  7–10 = craft inside known prog grammar
  11–14 = new-grammar behaviors
  15–18 = ordeal-capable prog
  19–20 = forbidden / event-level innovation
  21–23 = Icarus apex
- Confidence percentage.
- 2–4 sentence justification referencing SC, MI, BP, EG, CD, and HMII.

Step 9 — Quick Improvement Diagnosis

Give the most direct path upward:
- To gain +1 rung:
  What must improve?
- To gain +2–3 rungs:
  What architectural change is required?
- Biggest weakness:
  SC / MI / BP / EG / CD / HMII clarity.
- Biggest strength:
  Which axis currently carries the track?
